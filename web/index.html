Leading indicators

1. Net flow momentum

Who is absorbing whom. Buy/sell imbalance turns before price does, because it is what moves it.

2. Fee run-rate

All three fee legs, annualised. This is the protocol's revenue, and it is paid in AI, so it is also the burn engine.

3. Hard backing

NVDA accumulating in the vault. Unlike the burn, this is an asset that cannot be inflated away.

4. Hub conversion (κ)

Cross-routed AI volume ÷ direct AI volume. Swaps are grouped by transaction: one AI swap in a transaction is direct — someone wanted AI. Two or more, where the trader receives AI in one leg and spends it in another, means AI was a pass-through hop (BONER → AI → MEME); the routed amount is min(received, spent) and any residual counts as direct. It separates “people want to own AI” from “AI is the plumbing everything else moves through”. Only the second is the liquidity-hub thesis, and it is the more durable, because it does not need anyone to hold an opinion on AI at all.

5. Fee capture

Share of indexed AI volume crossing the tolled AI/NVDA pool. Revenue scales with this, so the hub's own success can cannibalise it.

6. Float removal

AI destroyed or immobilised, cumulatively. A slow, one-way squeeze on what can actually trade.

7. Cash-flow multiple, over time

Market cap divided by the annualised fee run-rate. Both scale with the AI price, so it cancels: this multiple is price-invariant and needs no oracle. Lower is cheaper.

8. Where the fees leak

Share of AI volume on fee-bearing (hooked) pools versus hookless ones. Anyone can open a v4 pool for AI with no hook and a lower fee; routers will prefer it, and the vault gets nothing.

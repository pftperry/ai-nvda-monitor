Where the measured inputs actually sit

Where the inputs actually sit

The structural inputs, measured on chain, each placed in its own measured range rather than against anyone's assumed scenarios. A row says whether this figure is high or low for this asset, lately — not what it will do next. The notes record how one circulating valuation writeup labelled the same inputs; that writeup prompted these questions but its cases are assumptions, and nothing on this page is scored against them.

AI in USD

Priced on chain against USDG, a USD stablecoin, so this is the pool's own execution price rather than an aggregator's opinion. Market cap is that price times live supply.

In dollars

Volume, fees and the vault in money rather than AI, each hour priced at that hour's on-chain AI/USDG close. NVDA's dollar price comes from the stock token's own USDG pool, cross-checked against the price AI's two pools imply for it. Turnover is volume over market cap; fee yield is what the toll pays holders, annualised, as a share of the cap. These are the figures a holder actually reasons in, and the ones the AI-denominated cards cannot show.

Liquidity depth

What it costs to move the price

Every other measure on this page is backward-looking: volume that happened, fees that were taken, flow that already crossed. This one faces forward. Concentrated liquidity sitting below spot is money already committed to buying AI; AI sitting above spot is supply already committed to selling. The gap between them says which direction is cheaper to push, before anyone trades. Rebuilt from every ModifyLiquidity event the pools have ever emitted, so it is measured rather than quoted — two checks confirm the replay: net liquidity across all ticks must come to exactly zero, and the liquidity active at the current tick must equal what the pool reports in its own last swap. Liquidity is not a promise. A position can be withdrawn in one block, so read this as the shape of the book right now, not as support that will be there later.

Leading indicators

1. Tokens launched per day

Tokens the LONG launchpad creates each day. Every pool carrying the LONG hook is a launchpad token and the hook address sits in the pool’s own Initialize log, so this is a census rather than a selection: it cannot be curated and it cannot be cherry-picked. It matters to AI because AI is the platform’s third most-used base pair, so the launchpad is the demand engine underneath the asset: mint volume feeds pool creation, pool creation feeds routing, and routing is what the fee is taken on. No average market cap per day, deliberately. Pricing a token costs two calls, so only the 300 most actively traded are priced. That leaves most days holding one or two priced tokens, and a single $295M outlier would make one July day outrank every other — a line about which token happened to survive into the sample, not about what launched. Platform-wide value is reported beside the count instead, where the sample it comes from can be named.

2. Near-spot depth imbalance

Which way is cheaper to push, measured where a trade can actually reach. Buying consumes the asks above spot; selling consumes the bids below it. More bids than asks means it costs less to move AI up than down. The full book above is drawn across ±50% because that is the shape worth seeing, but it is the wrong window for this question: depth half the price away cannot be touched by any trade that matters this week. These bands are summed straight from the tick ladders, not from the chart’s 0.83%-wide bins, which cannot resolve two percent. The gap between the two readings is itself the finding. Across ±50% this book is decisively bid-heavy; within ±2% it is level. The apparent support is parked out of range rather than standing under the price. Liquidity is not a promise. A position can be withdrawn in one block, so this is the shape of the book now, not a floor under it later.

3. Net flow momentum

Who is absorbing whom. Buy/sell imbalance moves price mechanically; measured, it does not lead it, so read it as the present rather than the future.

Distinct buying addresses per day

Breadth of demand: how many addresses bought, summed per pool-hour, so it is an upper bound on people. Volume can be one wallet; this cannot.

4. Fee run-rate

All three fee legs, annualised. This is the protocol's revenue, and it is paid in AI, so it is also the burn engine.

3b. Holder breadth and whales

Who is arriving, who is leaving, and what the large wallets are doing. Wallets above a fixed AI balance are the price-neutral count (a rally cannot manufacture one). Beneath it, the gross behind the net: wallets funded from zero and emptied to zero each day, replayed from every transfer. Concentration says whether size is spreading out or gathering in. The tape lists every move of 250,000 AI or more, classified by which side of it was the pool, so a reader can watch a wallet rather than a statistic. The Float & Lockup tab has the full tape, the largest wallets and the weekly cohorts.

3c. AI against its platform

Is AI leading or lagging the tokens launched around it? The census prices the platform's largest tokens on every slow-path run and keeps those prices as a series, so AI's move can be read against its own cohort's median rather than in isolation. A hub should lead when the ecosystem is bid; when the launches run ahead of the base pair they settle against, the money is going to them, not to AI.

5. Hard backing

NVDA accumulating in the vault. Unlike the burn, this is an asset that cannot be inflated away.

6. Hub conversion (κ)

Cross-routed AI volume ÷ direct AI volume. Swaps are grouped by transaction: one AI swap in a transaction is direct — someone wanted AI. Two or more, where the trader receives AI in one leg and spends it in another, means AI was a pass-through hop (BONER → AI → MEME); the routed amount is min(received, spent) and any residual counts as direct. It separates “people want to own AI” from “AI is the plumbing everything else moves through”. Only the second is the liquidity-hub thesis, and it is the more durable, because it does not need anyone to hold an opinion on AI at all.

6b. AI as a base pair — stock and flow

The hub thesis says other tokens will come to quote themselves in AI the way they quote themselves in ETH. That is a counting question and this counts it: across every pool the LONG launchpad has created, how often is each token chosen as the anchor. Cross-routing (above) measures volume passing through AI; this measures whether the platform is adopting AI as infrastructure, which is the slower and more durable version of the same claim.

The flow underneath it

The ranking above is a stock, and a stock accumulated since July cannot fall: it will read “third” for months after adoption stops. This is AI’s share of the pools created each day, which is the part that can turn, and it has — twice. A new pool anchored in AI must be seeded with AI before it can trade, so this is demand that does not require anyone to hold an opinion on AI, and it appears at pool creation while fees keep arriving from pools built weeks ago. Both terms are counted from the census by address, so unlike the rest of the Launchpad tab this ratio does not inherit the real-world-asset list’s undercount. What it is not: proof of open-market buying. A launcher may seed from AI already held, and a one-sided range needs little.

7. Fee capture

Share of indexed AI volume crossing the tolled AI/NVDA pool. Revenue scales with this, so the hub's own success can cannibalise it.

8. Float removal

AI destroyed or immobilised, cumulatively. A slow, one-way squeeze on what can actually trade.

9. Cash-flow multiple, over time

Market cap divided by the annualised fee run-rate. Both scale with the AI price, so it cancels: this multiple is price-invariant and needs no oracle. Lower is cheaper.

10. Where the fees leak

Share of AI volume on fee-bearing (hooked) pools versus hookless ones. Anyone can open a v4 pool for AI with no hook and a lower fee; routers will prefer it, and the vault gets nothing. Measured across the venues indexed in depth rather than all ~5,600 pools that hold AI: only the deep set has hourly volume, and it is chosen as the busiest, so it carries most of the flow but is not a census.

Who holds AI

Every AI transfer since genesis replayed into a balance per address, snapshotted every four hours. Nothing is sampled, and the balances must sum to supply exactly before any count is published.

How the labels and the score are defined

Holds 10%+ of its stock: the pool holds at least 10% of its stock's whole tokenized supply, carries at least 2¢ of stock per dollar of market cap, and turns over between 1% and 50% of its cap a day with a swap count that looks like people, not a loop. This is the stickiness mechanism itself: above 10% anyone who wants size in that stock on chain ends up trading through the pair.

Well-backed, active: at least 3% of the stock's supply, at least 6¢ of stock per dollar of cap, turnover between 10% and 50% with at least 100 swaps a day. Smaller caps with about twice the cushion, where the re-rating available is growing into a 10%+ holder of the stock, which AI, BONER and MEME each did.

Two scores. Stock-Based is the thesis profile: each pair's percentile rank among the rows shown, share of the stock's supply ×3, stock per dollar of cap ×2, turnover inside the 1%–50% band ×2, swaps ×1, stock in pools ×1. It says where a pair sits in the stickiness story, and the venues (AI, BONER, MEME, MOO) score highest. It is not a forecast: on the tape it ranked the next one, two and four weeks backwards in both samples (rank correlation −0.33, −0.45, −0.21 in the launch cohort; −0.18, −0.09, −0.17 among survivors), and it was written before the tape was seen, so that result is out of sample. Forward Looking is what did lead price.

Forward Looking (0–100): points from a base of 50, one term per shape, read intraday and refreshed every standard run (about every two hours): swaps in the trailing 24 hours more than 70% above the pool's average complete day of the last seven +20; trailing 24 hours more than half below the complete day three days back −10; the pool's stock level now against its close seven days ago, down more than 10% −25, up between 0% and 50% +15, up more than 50% 0; the pair's live dollar price against its close seven days ago, up between 0% and 30% +10, up more than 30% −10; at least 5¢ of stock per dollar of cap +5; last complete day's turnover between 1% and 50% of cap +5. A term the pair is too young to compute scores nothing. Share of the stock's supply and raw swap or volume counts carry no weight. Points rather than ranks, so one pair's score does not depend on how the rest of the field is doing that week.

Why those terms (backtest of 16 Sep 2026): every pair's own swap tape was rebuilt from its pool's creation into daily readings, and each reading was set against the pair's price in its stock seven days later. Two samples kept apart: a launch cohort of 38 pairs picked only on having 50+ swaps in their first week, whatever became of them (about a quarter of their weeks were up), and the survivors on this tab (about six in ten up). Levels did not help: share of stock preceded weaker weeks in every cut of both samples (rank correlation −0.26 to −0.44), and stock per dollar of cap was negative in the cohort and positive only among survivors, which is what survivorship looks like. Swap changes were one-sided: a surge of more than 70% was above base in both samples, a collapse of more than half was below base in the cohort, and modest declines were noise, so a modest decline is not penalised. A pool that lost more than a tenth of its stock in a week trailed in both samples (57% up against 68% among survivors, with a median week of +7% against +40%); steady accumulation of 0–50% led; a jump of more than 50% was strong among survivors and 0 for 12 in the cohort, so it scores nothing. A price already up more than 30% in seven days was 0 for 7 in the cohort and below base among survivors. The composite built from those terms was checked as a whole: among survivors the score bands run 50%, 54%, 57%, 65%, 68% up from bottom to top with median weeks from 0% to +46%, and in the cohort the two top bands were the only ones above base. On longer horizons the one level that earns its place is the cushion: stock per dollar of cap ran +0.24 at one and two weeks and +0.43 at four weeks among survivors, and turned positive at four weeks in the cohort too, while share and turnover stayed negative at every horizon. Forward Looking is +0.05 to +0.45 across horizons and samples, but it was designed after looking at this tape, so its numbers are in-sample and flattered; the Stock-Based result is the honest one. Modest, one month of history, one regime, small counts in the tails; the test reruns as the tape grows.

In dollars (retest of 16 Sep 2026): the tests above were first run with prices in shares of the stock, which isolates the pool but is not what a holder banks. With each stock's daily close from its Chainlink feed (or its USDG pools) every pair-day now has a dollar price, and the tests were rerun in USDG. Same answer, sharper. Stock-Based ranked the next week backwards in both samples (−0.34 in the launch cohort, −0.20 among survivors), and its top band was the worst place to be: pairs scoring 70+ on Stock-Based were up the following week 23% of the time in the cohort and 38% among survivors, with a median week of −8% and −19%, against 66% and 55–71% for the lower bands. Forward Looking ran +0.15 and +0.10 at one week and +0.47 and +0.06 at four, with its 75+ band up 67% (cohort) and 65% (survivors) of the following weeks. Term by term in dollars: a swap surge 61% up against 40% (cohort); steady accumulation 64% against 41% for shedding; a price up 0–30% on the week 58% against 29% for one already up 30%+. Cushion split the samples again, 10¢+ the best band among survivors (71% up, median +44%) and the worst in the cohort (35%), so it keeps its small weight. Dollar prices are mid prices; a round trip through the stock costs fees and slippage the tape cannot see.

Flags: turnover above 100% a day is bot volume (watch whether the stock stays when it fades); under 1% with fewer than 20 swaps is no trading; under 3% of the stock's supply is little stock. Own 30d is where today's backing sits among the pair's own readings over the last thirty days (one point per four hours); it needs a week of points before it says anything. Everything is measured on chain each standard run; the view on the underlying stock is yours.

What the words mean

Every term on this tab, in plain language. "The stock" is the tokenized share the pair trades against (NVDA for AI, HIMS for BONER); "the pool" is the pair's main LONG pool, which holds the meme token on one side and the stock on the other.

The two scores

Forward Looking
Points from a base of 50 for the shapes that came before better weeks in the backtest, in dollars. The Signals column shows which terms a pair is scoring on.
Stock-Based
The original thesis profile: how much of its stock the pair holds, how much stock backs each dollar of its market cap, and whether it trades like a market, ranked against the other rows. It describes what a pair is. In the backtest it ranked the next weeks backwards, so it sits beside Forward Looking rather than inside it.

Signals

Swap surge
Swaps in the trailing 24 hours are more than 70% above the pool's average day over the last week. Activity is picking up.
Swaps collapsing
The trailing 24 hours of swaps are less than half of the complete day three days back.
Accumulating
The pool holds between 0% and 50% more of the stock than at the close seven days ago. Buyers of the meme token pay in stock, so the pile grows when there is net buying.
Shedding stock
The pool holds more than 10% less of the stock than at the close seven days ago. Sellers of the meme token take stock out, so a week of net selling drains the pile, and the backing the Stock-Based score is built from is being withdrawn. The heaviest penalty, because it trailed in both samples.
Stock jumped
The pile grew more than 50% since the close seven days ago. It went well among the pairs that survived and badly among launches that did not, so it scores nothing either way.
Steady price
The pair's live dollar price is up between 0% and 30% on its close seven days ago.
Extended
The pair's live dollar price is up more than 30% on its close seven days ago. Buying after that run did worse than buying a steady one.
5¢+ cushion
At least five cents of stock in the pool for every dollar of the pair's market cap.
Trades like a market
On the last complete day, the stock traded through the pool was between 1% and 50% of the pair's market cap. Below that nobody is trading; above it the volume is machines.

The test

Pair-day
One pair on one complete UTC day with at least one swap. The unit the backtest counts.
Launch cohort
Pairs launched at least thirty days ago that had 50 or more swaps in their first week, kept whatever became of them. The sample with losers in it.
Survivors
The pairs on this tab today. By definition the launches that worked, so anything that looks good in this sample alone may just be survivorship.
Top band
Forward Looking 75 or better; Stock-Based 70 or better. The retest asks how often the following week was up for pairs in that band against all pairs.
Rank correlation
From −1 to +1: whether a higher score went with a higher return over the following days, without assuming a straight line. Zero is no relationship.
Out of sample
Pair-days dated after 16 September 2026, when Forward Looking's terms were fixed. The score never saw these weeks, so this is the only figure that can prove or break it.

What a base is made of

Concentration is one measure and a thin one on its own. A coin is held up by how many wallets there are, whether the ones with real size are growing, whether arrivals outnumber departures, and whether the people who bought stayed.

What each measure is

Top 10 / 50 / 100 is the share of holder-owned supply in that many largest wallets. The common heuristic is that under 50% in the top ten is healthy and over 70% is a warning; Bitcoin and Ethereum sit under 15%.

Nakamoto coefficient is how many wallets would have to act together to move more than half the supply. Higher is harder to corner.

Gini runs 0 (every wallet equal) to 1 (one wallet holds everything). Token holder sets read high because dust wallets are numerous, so the direction matters more than the level.

HHI is the concentration index competition regulators use, the sum of squared percentage shares. Above 2,500 counts as highly concentrated in antitrust terms.

Gini, HHI and the Nakamoto count are computed from every balance at the moment of each snapshot and begin from the run that introduced them; the top-ten, top-fifty and top-hundred series run back to launch.

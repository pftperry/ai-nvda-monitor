The screen: gates, tiers and the score

Venue pair: the pool holds at least 10% of its stock's whole tokenized supply, carries at least 2¢ of stock per dollar of market cap, and turns over between 1% and 50% of its cap a day with a swap count that looks like people, not a loop. This is the stickiness mechanism itself: above 10% anyone who wants size in that stock on chain ends up trading through the pair.

Challenger: at least 3% of the stock's supply, at least 6¢ of stock per dollar of cap, turnover between 10% and 50% with at least 100 swaps a day. Smaller caps with about twice the cushion, where the re-rating available is crossing into venue status, which AI, BONER and MEME each did.

Score (0–100): each pair's percentile rank among the rows shown, weighted: share of the stock's supply ×3, stock per dollar of cap ×2, turnover inside the 1%–50% band ×2, swaps ×1, stock in pools ×1. It is relative to the field, so it moves as the field moves; it rewards the profile (a pair that is the venue for its stock, cushioned, and actually trading) and says nothing about the stock itself.

Flags: turnover above 100% a day is a loop (machine volume; watch whether the stock stays when it fades); under 1% with fewer than 20 swaps is parked; under 3% of the stock's supply is thin. Own 30d is where today's backing sits among the pair's own readings over the last thirty days (one point per four hours); it needs a week of points before it says anything. Everything is measured on chain each standard run; the view on the underlying stock is yours.
